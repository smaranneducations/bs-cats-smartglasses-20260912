## Online business and international audience

All routine operation and customer delivery should be possible online. Build useful intelligence for an international audience, with market/variant labels where product claims require them. The country of a source or the financial owner's residence does not select the business's target audience.

A smaller catalogue with reusable attributes, practical buyer questions and affordable maintenance can be a better initial business than a much larger dataset. Compare the full recurring workload and switching cost before expanding or changing domains.

Digital content, intelligence reports/tools and disclosed online commercial relationships are candidate offers. Recommendations for physical goods can fit if fulfilment belongs to the retailer. In-person services, owned stock, shipping and country-bound dealer operations are not default dependencies.

Before ranking a domain, trace the buyer's path from a useful question to an online purchase and the business's path from that purchase to attributable net receipts. Document real offers, customer markets, checkout/fulfilment friction, publisher eligibility, payment/return conditions and recurring human effort. Source availability and sponsor categories alone cannot establish commercial fit. SmartGlasses remains the working domain while its online retail and monetization path is assessed; vehicle expansion is deferred.

## Intended outcome

Build a reusable intelligence platform for solo founders, validated through a small, trustworthy smart-glasses intelligence business. An operator combines personal expertise with agents to capture context and data, curate meaningful knowledge, produce analysis, and distribute or commercialize useful outputs with little administration. Optimize for sustained contribution after costs and reasonable owner time. Treat income and audience growth as hypotheses to test; there is no guaranteed revenue or completion date for a profitable business.

The initial asset is traceable product knowledge. It supports useful comparisons and original explainer videos. The first delivery is one complete, reviewed product-to-video-to-feedback journey. Expand coverage and monetization only after this journey works. This follows the blueprint's core architecture and acceptance criteria while bringing one small end-to-end example forward before bulk catalogue expansion.

## Core asset and product layers

The owner clarified that the platform, not a single channel, is the enduring product. Build three clear boundaries: the reusable intelligence core; domain metadata (SmartGlasses first); and presentation/distribution adapters. Domain metadata contains concepts, field definitions, relationships, source policies, applicable agent profiles and output configuration. Adding a new field should not require building another custom agent or form.

| Layer | What it contains | Human contribution and visible outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capture | Permitted evidence, observations, goals, context and object-level forms | The operator can add a fact, explain a constraint or supply a missing piece of context |
| Semantic knowledge | Definitions, ontology, relationships, normalized assertions, confidence and history | People and agents share the same meaning; every fact remains explainable |
| Curation and reasoning | Reusable agent roles, validation, proposals, analyses and dependency tracking | Agents improve and analyze objects within policy; humans resolve important uncertainty |
| Governance | Permissions, objectives, budgets, approvals, audit events and exception queues | The operator approves selected decisions and supervises authorized autonomous work |
| Presentation | Object pages, comparisons, charts, reports, kinetic videos and later commercial exports | One governed knowledge base supports multiple useful customer experiences |
| Distribution and learning | Channel adapters, external IDs, feedback, usage and commercial outcomes | Distribution results improve presentation; corrections improve knowledge separately |

Every important object includes a stable ID/type, purpose, current value, domain context, status/version, source or derivation, confidence where meaningful, permissions, related objects, discussion and change history. Its form includes type-specific fields plus space for useful human context. Capture the actor, timestamp, reason and previous version for edits; store opinions and observations under their own evidence type rather than promoting them directly to truth.

Human-in-the-loop means the owner reviews a proposal, supplies missing facts or approves the exact final output at a decision boundary. Human-on-the-loop means agents perform already-permitted work while the owner monitors results, adjusts objectives and policies, handles exceptions and can pause work. Implement both through the same governance model. The daily founder discussion is an operating habit; it does not substitute for object-level permissions or publication approval.

The initial platform operator is Bhasker. The initial audience consuming the domain outputs is smart-glasses buyers. Future solo founders who might pay to operate their own domains are a separate market hypothesis. Their onboarding, workspace isolation, data ownership, paid plans and support requirements belong to a later validated SaaS phase. Reusable contracts and metadata come first; a full marketplace or general-purpose multi-tenant product does not belong in the first $500 milestone.

A minimal reuse demonstration is to add a field through approved ontology/type metadata and have the form, validation and an applicable data view recognize it without a one-off UI or agent service. Show one approved fact flowing into both a comparison/report view and a video claim map. This is stronger evidence of the core asset than a standalone video-generation script.

## Business focus and positioning

Start with English-language buyers and curious users trying to understand what different smart glasses actually do. This is an operating assumption, not measured market demand. Lead with distinctions that change a buying decision: display versus camera/audio glasses, phone and subscription dependencies, compatibility, practical trade-offs, regional availability and what remains unknown.

An article or video must answer a concrete question better than a list of vendor specifications. Explain the consequence of the facts and link the supporting evidence. Do not imply that we physically tested a product unless an actual test is recorded. Distinguish advertised specifications, owner observations, independent measurements and our interpretation.

Start on YouTube with a corresponding evidence page. Prepare LinkedIn adaptations for professional context after the first workflow succeeds. Add more channels only if a supported adapter and audience evidence justify the work. No paid ads during initial validation.

Use the twelve workflow families and shared visual primitives in `.local/governance/WORKFLOW_CATALOG_V1.md`: category, product, feature, matched comparison, use case, value/ownership, change/launch, how-to/compatibility, audience questions, myth/conflict, market briefing and transparent commercial content. These are configurable workflows over shared evidence, not twelve isolated services or a mandate to publish every format immediately. The current creative default is 30-90 seconds of kinetic typography over original, authorized pictures, with background music and without mechanical voiceover; the canonical master is 9:16.

"Evidence over hype" is the current editorial promise, not a claim of slogan uniqueness or a restriction to dry, exhaustive research. Facts, reasoned opinion and qualified early signals may all help when clearly distinguished. Preserve security, legality, credibility, correction lineage and disclosed commercial independence while experimenting with engaging presentation and legitimate offers. Optimize for useful audience decisions, sustainable contribution and low human effort.

YouTube requires original, authentic value and distinguishes reusable presentation elements from repetitive or mass-produced substance. A consistent visual library is compatible with this plan only when each story adds meaningful analysis. Monetization remains subject to eligibility and review. ## Economics and experiments

Track costs independently: existing subscription, incremental coding credits, application-model API usage, voice/audio, rendering, cloud storage and egress, database usage, domain/email, payment fees and support effort. Show both incremental cash margin and the fuller cost including an allocated share of fixed subscriptions. Do not treat the existing subscription as free or as an API allowance.

For each piece record production cost, research reuse, owner review minutes, revisions, impressions, qualified visits, returning viewers, inquiry counts and collected revenue where attributable. Record unknown analytics as unavailable; never replace them with invented estimates.

`Contribution = collected attributable revenue - refunds - transaction/platform fees - attributable variable operating costs`.

`Operating surplus before tax = total contribution - fixed operating expenses`.

If contribution per sale is positive, `break-even sales = fixed expenses / contribution per sale`, rounded up. State allocation choices and avoid double-counting. Tax, contractual and payout choices are owner decisions; do not create personal financial forecasts from view counts.

Evaluate initial audience experiments over a suggested four-week window, allowing longer for low exposure. Compare similar formats and enough observations; a single viral or poorly distributed video is weak evidence. Do not invent universal CTR or retention targets. Establish a baseline, change one meaningful variable, and preserve factual quality.

If demand is weak, narrow the question or improve usefulness before raising output volume. If people engage but do not convert, test the offer and relevance before building more features. If quality or margin deteriorates, reduce cadence and fix the bottleneck. Increase spending only within an approved allowance and when evidence shows a plausible return.

## Architecture

| Component | Initial responsibility | Simplicity decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Firebase web app and Auth | Public comparisons, owner review, identity and roles | One shared application, with server-enforced admin permissions |
| Firestore | Tasks, leases, proposals, approvals, comments, charter, model policy and workflow state | Explicit documents and states; no separate workflow platform |
| BigQuery | Ontology, products, assertions, evidence metadata, versions and metrics | Use the existing dataset first with logical table groups; bounded parameterized queries |
| Cloud Storage | Private evidence, render inputs, final media and derived public assets | Start with the existing assets bucket; prefix/access separation; no duplicate bucket merely for naming consistency |
| Python API and reusable agent runtime | Domain tools, research proposals, orchestration and model routing | One deployment initially; roles are metadata, not independent always-on agents |
| Renderer | HTML/CSS/JS primitives, Playwright capture and FFmpeg composition | One bounded batch worker; preview before final output |
| Publisher adapter | Authorized upload, metadata, external IDs and later metrics | Isolate credentials and permissions; split deployment only when needed |
| Scheduler | Bounded due work and metric refresh after controls exist | No high-frequency speculative crawling or perpetual agent polling |

The existing Firestore database is in `us-central1`. Keep dependent resources compatible while documenting regional implications before storing sensitive contributor data. Do not recreate the database or change region for stylistic consistency.

The codebase may be public while private data stays access-controlled. Git ignore rules alone do not provide data security. Backend authorization, private storage, safe logs, container exclusions and a pre-push secret check are part of the design. A public build must work with dummy configuration and must not require another owner's credentials.

Development and the daily Codex adviser use this Mac. The blueprint's production pipeline should ultimately run in Cloud Run independently of the laptop, after deployment permissions, secret handling and numeric operating limits are resolved. Keep those two execution environments distinct.

## Harness and controlled autonomy

Every job carries `task_id`, objective, actor, charter/policy version, input versions, evidence references, output schema, acceptance criteria, risk, permitted tools, deadline, idempotency key and resource reservation. Fail closed on missing authority or budget for an external action; continue unrelated local work.

Use a shared queue with states `pending`, `running`, `needs_review`, `succeeded`, `failed` and `blocked`. Acquire a lease before work; recover expired leases without duplicating externally visible actions. Writes use version checks, immutable history and attributable decisions. Source material cannot change policy or tool permissions.

Use these gates in order:

1. Confirm source policy and rights before automated collection or publication use.
2. Normalize values, regions and product variants; retain evidence, freshness and confidence reasons.
3. Validate schemas, units, contradictions and historical consistency with deterministic checks first.
4. Propose uncertain/high-impact changes for review; never resolve uncertainty by fabrication.
5. Produce a script with explicit factual claims and a complete evidence map.
6. Assemble a typed visual manifest from tested primitives and rights-cleared assets.
7. Check rendering, captions, audio, disclosures, source attribution and technical channel constraints.
8. Obtain approval bound to the exact artifact hash and metadata version. Any material edit invalidates that approval.
9. Recheck permissions, quotas, duplicates and approval before upload; retain resumable state and the external ID.
10. Collect available metrics for that exact creative recipe; preserve editorial feedback separately from popularity.

Publishing approval is retained because the blueprint explicitly requires it. Minimize effort with one batch review player showing the final video, claims, rights notes, title and description. Routine implementation, proposed research and draft rendering proceed automatically inside the policy. Notify the owner only when a consequential decision is prepared or an unavoidable consent is needed.

Testing covers the boundaries: invalid provenance, prompt injection from sources, unauthorized writes, changed-artifact approval reuse, duplicate uploads, cost reservation races, exhausted retries and interrupted rendering. Use recorded fixtures and mocks for routine work so checks do not publish or consume paid APIs. Add representative media QA and small agent evaluations when those components exist.

## Model policy and diversity of judgment

The initial policy is in `config/model-routing.json`. The local selector reads task type, risk, failure count, model availability and optional usage pressure. It returns a recommendation and limits without making any model/API call. It does not enforce the production budget or switch the current conversation's model.

| Work | Initial selection | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parsing, metric arithmetic, known validation, frame capture, encoding | Deterministic tools | Repeatable work does not need generation |
| Small documentation changes, classification and bounded extraction | GPT-5.6 Luna, medium reasoning | Small, constrained work with inspectable output |
| Ordinary feature work, integrations and evidence-grounded drafts | GPT-5.6 Terra, medium reasoning | Default implementation tier |
| Security boundaries, factual review, cost controls, publishing gates | GPT-5.6 Sol, high reasoning | Greater consequence of a subtle error |
| Cross-system architecture and difficult diagnosis | GPT-6 Astra, high reasoning | Higher reasoning demand; use for a bounded decision |

These are initial routing hypotheses using models listed by this app, not a claim that every API account supports the same identifiers. Validate production-provider names, access, pricing and task quality before adding live routes. Do not put unverified per-token prices into the budget engine. [OpenAI model selection](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/model-selection).

For a new consequential task family, establish a small trusted reference set and compare accepted results. Prefer the cheapest route that meets the quality threshold including retries and review. Do not equate a model's self-reported confidence with correctness. Keep a stronger quality floor for security and publication controls; when capacity is unavailable, defer those actions instead of silently weakening the gate.

Review from different perspectives through explicit questions: Does a buyer learn something useful? Does the evidence support it? Is the implementation recoverable? What does it cost to operate? Can one owner maintain it? Use a second independent reviewer for consequential changes when justified. Multiple models are not proof of truth, and a standing multi-agent debate is unnecessary overhead.

Three attempts per bounded task are the initial maximum: first attempt, one targeted repair, one escalation if the failure is about reasoning. Authentication, absent evidence and unavailable API scopes bypass this cycle to their proper resolution. Default to one worker and allow two only for independent, disjoint work. Record accepted output, model, tokens, tool calls, retries, wall time and estimated/actual cost separately.

The daily founder check-in is registered in this task as `smartglasses-model-and-cost-adviser` for 09:00 app-local time, intended as Europe/Zurich. It includes task fit, cost concerns, progress and up to three prepared partner questions. It does not deploy, spend or publish. It uses the task's configured model unless the app supports an explicit override. Local scheduled work requires the computer and app to remain running. [Scheduled tasks](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/automations?surface=app).

Subscription use is finite and depends on workload and model. API-key usage has separate usage-based pricing. Do not infer the user's exact allowance from the previously mentioned subscription price, or automatically buy credits when capacity runs low. [OpenAI pricing and usage](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing).

## Cost envelope and enforcement plan

Blaze and its selected billing account were authorized earlier. The owner subsequently authorized $500 total for the first experiment, including the $80 subscription paid on 2026-09-12, APIs, hosting and all other costs. This is a cumulative ceiling with no monthly reset. The remaining amount is at most $420 before other charges. The earlier $5 alert creation failed; neither that amount nor the new configuration file is an installed provider cap. New recurring paid jobs remain unarmed until costs are reconciled and admission controls are tested. Existing cloud assets may already incur usage or retention costs.

Before unattended paid execution, implement a central reservation ledger for task, day and the entire experiment; monthly views are reports, not a renewed allowance. Estimate worst-case tokens, voice duration, render time, query bytes, storage and egress using current verified prices. Atomically reserve capacity before dispatch, reconcile actual usage and actual invoices without duplication, expire reservations safely, and reject unknown prices, stale accounting or oversubscription. A local-only ledger is insufficient for concurrent distributed workers.

Use internal variable-cost limits of $2 per job and $5 per day while always respecting remaining experiment headroom. Review at $150, $250 and $350 total exposure; pause new paid work at $450 and protect $50 for late/fixed charges. Record subscription renewals/upgrades inside the same $500 ceiling, and do not purchase them automatically. See the scorecard for attribution and decision rules.

Initial engineering limits to implement and tune through measurement: one render at a time; three model attempts per task; two maximum parallel development workers; previews before final renders; bounded query bytes; per-source fetch limits; maximum job duration; and disabled paid generators until an allowance exists. These limits are design requirements until the runtime enforces and tests them.

Keep an alerts-only project budget for visibility and consider provider spend-cap budgets where the account/service supports them. Google currently documents service-scoped spend caps in preview, including eligible Cloud Run and AI services. Their enforcement can lag, in-flight work may finish, and persistent charges can continue. They supplement application controls and do not establish a universal zero-overrun guarantee. [Google Cloud spend caps](https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/budgets-spend-caps).

Cache normalized evidence and approved media. Schedule by freshness needs: unchanged reference specifications need fewer checks than launches or prices. Batch metrics queries; partition analytical data. Avoid always-on compute, speculative API loops and automatic final-render retries. Retain learning records while setting deliberate policies for bulky intermediate frames; do not silently delete authoritative evidence.

## Review rhythm and stop conditions

At each material task change, assess model fit and update the checkpoint. The daily founder check-in includes progress and useful questions, plus meaningful routing or cost issues without repeating unchanged blockers. Once publication is live, review aggregate content performance weekly and business viability monthly; implement production schedules only when data, budgets and permissions are ready.

The weekly owner view should fit on one screen: delivered items, next useful outcome, actual or unavailable spend, audience/inquiry/revenue evidence, corrections and one bundled review queue. Aim for a small batch-review session rather than repeated operational requests; measure the burden before promising a specific time saving.

Pause only the affected action when evidence, rights, approval, authorization or budget is missing. Use fixtures, drafts, previews, manual kits and independent implementation work to keep progressing. Pause scale-up if repeated errors or poor economics outweigh progress. Resume from durable checkpoints; do not rely on an unlimited chat session or an always-awake laptop.

# Canonical content and distribution

## One content object

The platform creates one atomic vertical video, not a different video for each channel. Every unit answers one buyer question, explains one product attribute and its practical impact, compares one attribute across two products, or explains one dated news event. Evidence caveats and attribution remain visible but do not expand the unit into additional topics.

The master is a self-hosted 9:16 MP4 lasting 30-90 seconds. The owned application is the durable source of truth. YouTube and LinkedIn receive replicas; losing either account must not remove the application asset. A poster, captions, evidence manifest and platform-copy set are derivatives of the same canonical version.

## One orchestrated workflow

Strategist, evidence researcher, marketer, script writer, media curator, editor, director, renderer, release reviewer and publisher are bounded task stages. They share the same versioned context and media catalog. They are not independent content generators and cannot silently replace each other's approved output.

The strategist selects one audience question. Evidence work binds current claims and unknowns. The marketer creates a truthful hook and channel framing. The writer produces one script. The media curator reuses the catalog before lawful acquisition or bounded generation. The editor and director refine the same package. The renderer emits one canonical video. The publisher receives credentials only after exact approval.

## Distribution

- Owned app: publish the canonical self-hosted asset after exact approval. First-party likes, dislikes, comments and reports attach to its canonical content ID.
- YouTube: upload the exact video by API with pinned title, description, tags, disclosure and app link.
- LinkedIn: upload the exact video by API with pinned professional framing, disclosure and app link.
- Instagram, TikTok and X: prepare a manual kit containing the same video, platform-specific text, hashtags and canonical app link.

The platform-specific copy may differ because audience context and length differ. Factual meaning, disclosures and video bytes may not drift.

## Approval and safety

One publication request pins video, poster and caption hashes; evidence and media-rights results; the self-hosted URL; every destination; and every exact title, description, post or tag set. Approval applies only to that snapshot. A changed byte, claim, disclosure, destination or caption requires another request.

YouTube and LinkedIn execution can be automatic only after this exact request is approved and their adapters validate current platform requirements. Ambiguous uploads are reconciled before retry.

First-party engagement requires authentication or bounded abuse controls, moderation, reporting, deletion and audit attribution. YouTube comments are not treated as the canonical engagement database and are not required for the owned discussion experience.

Owned video delivery can create bandwidth cost. Measure storage and egress per accepted view, use cacheable compressed renditions and retain the original governed master without generating separate channel videos.
